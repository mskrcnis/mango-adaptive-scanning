# Literature and claims review for the dataset-only paper

Reviewed 26 November 2026. This is a bounded primary-source and claim audit for a retrospective empirical paper, not a systematic review or an originality certification. The existing bibliography was copied into this revision; earlier manuscripts and source artifacts were not edited. All 33 entries in `../../data/literature/source_manifest.json` matched their recorded SHA-256 values during this review.

## Scope decision

`scope.md` is consistent with the evidence and the user's dataset-only completion criterion. Seasons 4 and 5 are held-out seasons **within the existing public dataset**. Prefer that phrase to an unqualified “external validation,” which could imply a newly collected independent cohort. Their outcomes were excluded from the original frozen model/operating-point selection, but the benchmark had prior local use; later development-only analyses are exploratory follow-ups. They must not be retrospectively described as preregistered confirmation or promoted using held-season results.

Published manufacturer emission profiles remain fixed inputs to the existing spectral-response simulation. They are typical digitized curves, not measurements of devices built for this study. The assumed base-10 apparent-absorbance conversion, flat detector/reference weighting, finite plotted support and normalization belong in Methods and limitations. A simulated reading count is not measured energy, scan duration, manufacturing cost or achieved hardware savings. There is no need to collect new spectra or build an instrument to complete this retrospective paper. Physical transfer and new-cohort work are optional future research, not missing stages of this paper.

Dry matter is the measured target. Harvest timing can motivate the work, but no pick/wait labels, decision threshold, prospective orchard intervention or postharvest outcomes were analyzed. Exclude claims that the model optimizes harvest timing, makes validated picking recommendations, improves fruit quality or establishes agronomic utility.

## Suggested introduction and related-work wording

The following short paragraphs state supportable claims. Citation keys are ready for the manuscript writer.

> Near-infrared spectra have been used to estimate intact-mango dry matter content, with variation across growing seasons, locations and cultivars motivating robustness studies (`anderson2020`). The associated public dataset provides a reproducible setting for comparing prediction methods across recorded seasons (`andersondata2024`). CNN methods have also been studied on this benchmark, although preprocessing and fitting choices affect the comparison (`walsh2024`, `walshthesis2024`). Here, dry matter prediction is the observed task; harvest timing is an application motivation rather than an evaluated decision outcome.

> Reducing spectral input can mean either choosing idealized wavelengths or simulating finite optical responses. Gutiérrez and colleagues already used realistic filter-response simulation and filter-combination search for mango dry-matter estimation (`gutierrez2019`). Active wavelength selection also predates this study in infrared mixture analysis (`huang2015`). These precedents motivate comparisons between fixed acquisitions and adaptive acquisitions, while keeping the physical assumptions of each simulation explicit.

> Dynamic feature acquisition selects further measurements from information already observed. Covert et al. relate learned greedy acquisition to expected one-step prediction loss (`covert2023`), while Valancius et al. consider additional subsets through a loss-plus-cost objective (`valancius2024`). Supervised early-exit risk also compares the loss of an intermediate prediction with that of a full model (`jazbec2024`). Our contribution is an empirical evaluation of these related choices in a fixed mango spectral simulation: how the reference prediction, partial predictor and acquisition order affect accuracy at the achieved reading count. We do not claim that loss differences, subset selection or adaptive stopping are new algorithms.

Avoid comparing the cited papers' published RMSEPs or R-squared values with local scores as if they were a matched experiment. Their targets, sample preparation, preprocessing, splits and available inputs differ or are incompletely documented. Author-architecture adaptations and restricted lookahead must be labeled beside their results.

## Claims that the manuscript should enforce

- Describe the original frozen held-season analysis separately from the subsequent v2/v3 development-only investigations. Disclose repeated use of the development data and prior local use of the public benchmark.
- State achieved mean reading counts with accuracy differences. A lower error at a larger acquisition budget does not establish a better error-versus-reading tradeoff. Give the prespecified comparability and engineering-screen outcome as observed, including failure or inconclusive results.
- Call the bank and rich-spectrum references **fitted predictors**. The identity `e_partial - e_rich = (e_partial - e_bank) + (e_bank - e_rich)` is an algebraic decomposition. Its second term can include estimator, preprocessing and fitting effects; it is not an experimentally isolated physical information limit.
- Treat the final six-channel candidate's minimum cross-validation selection score as tuning evidence. Do not assign earlier fold-specific policies' held-season scores to this one final model, or present software parity as a new accuracy result.
- Preserve population-level uncertainty limits: season 5 has two populations; no population-level confidence interval is justified there by these analyses. Random-seed variability is not a substitute for sampling uncertainty. Exploratory conditional intervals do not imply multiplicity-controlled confirmation.
- Use “digitized manufacturer profiles,” “simulated finite-band readings,” and “held-out seasons within the dataset.” Exclude “measured LED channels,” “validated hardware saving,” “new independent cohort,” “risk-guaranteed under seasonal shift,” “first adaptive spectrometer,” and “validated harvest recommendation.”
